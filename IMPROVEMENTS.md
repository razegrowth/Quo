# What Quo does better than Search

Everything Quo has added to or fixed in [Search](https://github.com/driceroland/Search) since forking it, kept up to date as Quo changes. Newest first within each section. Naming and branding (Quo's name, icon, `quo://`, its own data) aren't listed.

## Quo for iPhone

- **Apple Intelligence on the iPhone**, where the phone has it and it's turned on; none of it appears where it isn't, and nothing it reads leaves the phone. **Shorter tab names** in the tab switcher ("Re: Q3 budget review" for "Re: Q3 budget review (4) - ed@example.com - Gmail"), on by default. **A summary under a held link's preview**: two or three sentences of what the page says, read without your cookies, and again with that site's own only if it turns a stranger away, never carried across a redirect. **Hold a card in the tab switcher** for what its page says without opening it. **Ask This Page** in the ^ toolbar: questions about the page in front, answered from its own words as they're made, following on from each other. Settings › Shorter tab names and Summaries in link previews. *Nov 2026*
- **Listen to Page on the iPhone**: the article read aloud by the phone's best voice for its language, from the ^ toolbar, going on with Quo in the background or the phone locked, with a Live Activity on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island to pause or stop it, and play and pause in Control Centre. A bar over the page while it's read. Going elsewhere in the tab, or closing it, stops it. *Nov 2026*
- **Questions go to your answer engine, site names to the site**: on the iPhone, something you ask ("how do I boil an egg", "what's the capital of France?") goes to Perplexity or ChatGPT if you choose one in Settings › Search Engine › Questions go to, and with none chosen, asking one is the top suggestion; a site's name on its own ("github", "figma", one you've been to or pinned) goes to the site. Everything else is a search, as before. Decided on the phone as you type. *Nov 2026*
- **Siri, Spotlight and Shortcuts on the iPhone**: Summarise This Page, Read This Aloud, Send My Tabs to My Mac and Open in Quo, each ready by voice with nothing to set up ("Summarise this page in Quo"). *Nov 2026*
- **History found by what it was about**: the iPhone's search field suggests pages you've visited whose titles mean what you typed, after the ones that match it word for word ("cheap flights to lisbon" finds "TAP Air Portugal: Book cheap tickets", "melting polar ice" an article on Arctic sea ice). Worked out on the phone, with no Apple Intelligence needed. *Nov 2026*
- **Clearer names for downloads called IMG_2834.pdf**: a file that arrives with a name that says nothing gets a suggestion from its own text (read on the phone, a scan or photo too) in the Downloads list, "Acme Supplies invoice March 2026.pdf", taken with a tap or ignored, never renamed on its own. Apple Intelligence only. *Nov 2026*
- **History synced between the iPhone and your Macs**: the pages you visit on one come up as you type on the other, through your own iCloud, the last 60 days. What you clear goes everywhere: Clear History, the last hour or the last day, or a page removed on the Mac, forgotten on your other devices too and never handed back, while a page you visit again afterwards stays. Never an Incognito page. Settings › Sync › Sync history, on while sync is; off takes the iPhone's history back out of iCloud. *Nov 2026*
- **Incognito tabs lock when you leave Quo for iPhone**, as Safari and Chrome's do: come back and an incognito page shows a lock in its place until Face ID (or Touch ID, or your passcode) says it's you, which Quo asks for straight away; See other tabs gets you to the rest meanwhile. In the tab switcher and on the bar's tab button an incognito tab shows a lock, not its page, and on the way out it's covered at once so the app switcher's picture of Quo doesn't show it either. Settings › Lock Incognito tabs with Face ID, on by default; switching it off while locked asks for Face ID too. *Nov 2026*
- **Clear the last hour, the last day, or everything** on the iPhone: Settings › Clear browsing data takes the history, recently closed tabs, and cookies and site data from that long ago on, so only the sites you were on then sign you out; everything also closes your tabs and empties the archive. *Nov 2026*
- **Bring back a tab you closed on the iPhone**: flick one away and Undo puts its card back where it was; hold + in the bar or the tab switcher for the last few closed, or Show All for the list (the last 30), each coming back where it was with its back and forward history. Incognito ones can be undone but are never listed or written down. *Nov 2026*
- **Downloads on the iPhone that work, and land in Files**: a file a page can't show, or one its server sends to be saved, downloads with the tab's own sign-ins and the page stays where it was (before, the tab sat loading and nothing came); a PDF or anything else shown as it is gets Download in the ^ toolbar, and every link Download Linked File in its long press. A notice says when it's started and when it's done, with Open to see it in Quick Look. The Downloads list (Settings, or the toolbar while something's coming in) shows each one's progress and size, opens it, shares it, tries again one that didn't finish, and clears the lot. They're all in the Files app too, under On My iPhone › Quo. *Nov 2026*
- **Send a page to your Mac, or to your iPhone**: on the iPhone, Send to and the Mac's name in the ^ toolbar and in a link's long-press menu; on the Mac, Send to and the iPhone's name in the tab's menu, at the top of File › Share…, as its own item in the File menu and as Send to Device in the command bar (to the device heard from last), a menu of them when there are several. The page opens on the other device once: on a Mac within half a minute, in front of a window of the same profile, with a note saying which device sent it ("From Edin's iPhone"); on the iPhone behind the page you're on, with a notice that takes you to it, or in front when Quo wasn't open. It goes in the sender's own tabs record through your own iCloud, end to end encrypted, written at once, and waits up to three days for a device that's away; never from a private or Incognito tab, a pin, or a tab an agent opened or works in. A Quo from before passes it over. *Nov 2026*
- **Video and music keep playing when you leave Quo for iPhone**, as they would in a music app: pages are never told they're hidden, so sites that stop when you switch apps or lock the phone play on, and whatever WebKit paused on the way out starts again; a video's sound is heard with the silent switch on. Settings › Keep playing when you leave Quo. **Picture in picture** from the ^ toolbar while the page has a video, even on sites that hide their own button. *Nov 2026*
- **Hold a link on the iPhone to see its page**: a live preview of the page, loaded with the tab's own sign-ins, and Open in New Tab, Open in Background, Open in Incognito, Copy Link, Share and Send to your Mac, with WebKit's own Save and Copy for an image under the link; a tap on the preview opens the page in the tab. *Nov 2026*
- **Your open tabs, handed between the iPhone and your Macs**: Quo for iPhone's spaces sheet lists, under each space, the tabs open in it on each Mac ("Open on" the Mac's name, and when it last said), a tap from opening here; your iPhone's tabs, and your other Macs', come up in the Mac's ⌘T field with the device's name, the few you looked at last before you type and the ones that answer to what you type after. Each device says what it has open as it is, through your own iCloud, end to end encrypted, at most every twenty seconds and at once as you leave it for the other; never a private or incognito tab, a pin, or a tab an agent opened or works in. A Quo from before passes them over. *Nov 2026*
- **An iPhone browser that keeps up with Quo on your Mac** (`iOS/`), in the spirit of Arc Search and with none of its AI. The page fills the screen over a slim bar: your tabs, + for a new search and ^ for the toolbar, the bar itself growing taller into each and shrinking back, as in Arc Search, its buttons giving way and its + stretching into the search field; a drag up on the bar grows it into a new search under the finger, and a sheet pulled down shrinks back into the bar; the bar slides away as you scroll down and straight back as you scroll up. Pull the page down from its top to load it again: a small frosted disc with the Q comes down into the gap, a ring drawing itself round it as you pull, a tick you feel once it's far enough, and the ring running round over the page's top while it loads. A new search rides up on the keyboard as one piece, on the keyboard's own curve, the keyboard starting the moment you tap + or let go of the bar, and goes back down with it into the bar the same way, the page staying as it is behind it, the + stretching into the field, suggests your open tabs, pins, bookmarks, history and the search engine's own as you type, takes your voice, and has an Incognito switch with its own memory-only sign-ins. The toolbar has back and forward, the address to copy or edit, Find on Page, Pin and Share, Translate (on the iPhone, by Apple's Translation), Reader, page zoom, the desktop site, the site's own settings and how many ads and trackers were blocked, with the original page a tap away. Tabs are a deck of cards as in Arc Search: the page shrinks into its card, the tab before it in the middle, a card tapped grows back into the page, and one flicked up closes. The tab button shows the tab you were on before, and holding it and dragging up brings that tab out under your finger as a card that grows as it rises, its own page, live, the page in front stepping back onto the dark; let go high, or flick it, and it fills the screen, let go low and it drops back in. The last few tabs you looked at stay ready behind the one in front, so going back to one never shows a blank page or waits for it to draw. Tabs left alone for a day, a week or a month go to an Archive, a tap from coming back. Your Mac's spaces, pins and bookmarks come through your own iCloud, end to end encrypted, in a panel washed with each space's colour, one space a swipe from the next, and what you pin on the iPhone appears on the Mac. Ads, trackers and cookie banners are blocked inside WebKit with EasyList, EasyPrivacy and Fanboy's Cookie Monster, turned into WebKit's rules by the Mac's own converter, per site. Everything moves at 120 frames a second on ProMotion iPhones, and what a finger lets go of carries on at the finger's speed, as Apple's own sheets do. The first launch opens in the dark, the Q blooming out of it with one fine ring spreading away, then three scenes you swipe through, each a small moving picture of what Quo does (a page losing its ads, tabs flying from a Mac to the phone, the tab card hopping out of the bar), and it opens onto the browser through a circle widening from the button. In Quo's greys, light and dark, with the Q on a white tile or an ink one. Search has no iPhone app. *Nov 2026*

## Looks and feel

- **The column of icons keeps the window's buttons where a Mac has them**: with Icons only, the three buttons sit side by side at the start of the address bar's row, the bar a touch tighter, and the column starts under it, on either side and with the bar on or off. Search stacked them one above the other at the column's top. *Nov 2026*
- **Settings, simpler**: each section shows what most people come for, around half the rows it had, and folds the rest under Advanced at its foot, in the same groups, remembered per section. Find a setting still finds every one, and opens the fold when it has to. Rows lose their icon tiles, and with Roomy the switches, buttons, choices and menus in them are a size up, as the rows are. Search shows every switch at once, small. *Nov 2026*
- **Site icons on from the start**: tabs and pins show each site's own icon unless you choose letters (Settings › Appearance). Search starts with letters. *Nov 2026*
- **⌘T and ⌘L set the page back rather than washing it out**: the page behind the field is dimmed and very slightly blurred, in Light as in Dark. Search laid white over it in Light. *Nov 2026*
- **Motion that answers at once and lands without a wobble**: following Apple's Designing Fluid Interfaces and Emil Kowalski's design-engineering rules, the interface's springs are critically damped, so a highlight, a card or a selection settles straight into place instead of overshooting and coming back; overshoot is kept for what the hand throws. Every button now gives a little under the pointer (97%, never a bounce). Nothing eases in any more: the column sliding back, a tooltip going and the little window closing all leave on a strong ease-out, shorter than they came. Nothing grows from almost nothing: badges, cards and controls start from 90% size. And what the keyboard does isn't animated at all: ⌘T and ⌘L's field opens and closes at once, and the arrow keys light their row at once, as Spotlight and Raycast do. Search's own motion overshot on most moves, eased some exits in, and animated the field on every ⌘L. *Nov 2026*
- **A new space comes in smoothly**: + at the column's foot fades and settles the tabs and pins away and brings the new space's card in a part at a time; Cancel and Create go back the same way. The card is a size up with Roomy. Search slid the whole column sideways and snapped. *Nov 2026*
- **The bookmarks list rises from the column's foot as a card**, with no popover arrow bent into the window's corner; a click anywhere else or Escape puts it away. *Nov 2026*
- **The space's Colour… picker is a size up with Roomy**: taller choices, larger words and a larger grain slider. *Nov 2026*
- **Every space at the column's foot, as in Arc**: each space's icon in a row across the foot, the one you're in lit in its colour on a ground that slides to the next as you switch, a click away from any of them, with + for a new one at the right. The foot's buttons are a size up with Roomy. With more spaces than fit, the one space's button as before. Search had a single small button that opened a menu. *Nov 2026*
- **The column lines up with the page**: with the address bar up, the pinned squares, or the first row when there are none, start exactly where the page's card does, and the window's buttons sit on the address field's line, in Roomy and Compact. They were 4 points low in Roomy and 12 in Compact. *Nov 2026*
- **An empty tab is your space's home**: the card in the space's colour and grain, its glyph, name and today's date over the field, and under it the sites you visit most, named as they name themselves, and the tabs you closed last, a click away. They step aside while you type. Search's empty tab was the field on a blank page. *Nov 2026*
- **A space's colour on a wheel, as in Arc**: the space's menu › Colour… is a colour wheel where you drag one, two or three handles to pick a colour or a gradient, with + and − for a handle more or less and an arrow to turn it. Handles snap to the ready-made hues and spring as they go, and the window shows the look live as you drag. A grain slider lays film grain over any space's colour, and the window's Light, Dark or Auto is a click away there too. Search had rows of swatches, a folded custom section and a grain switch for gradients only. *Nov 2026*
- **The small window for links, as Arc's Little Arc**: larger, it springs open, fades away, and kept, flies into its place in the browser's window; the page is a card on a frame in the space's colour; its bar has the site's icon, the site and the rest of the address dimmed, Back once you follow a link, Copy Link, a light while it loads, and Open in the space you're on, with the others on an arrow beside it. Search's had the site's name and Open in Search. *Nov 2026*
- **An agent's tab group looks like one**: the group Claude or another agent makes to work in shows the agent's icon before its name, a soft wash of colour and a thread down the side of its tabs, and, while the agent is working there, moving dots and a light passing over the heading. Your own groups look as before. *Nov 2026*
- **Group headings fit Roomy**: taller, with larger names, so they sit in proportion with Roomy's taller tabs instead of looking squeezed between them, and a folded group shows its fold mark. *Nov 2026*

Six full-window steps on the window's own black, which takes on the colour, gradient and grain you pick as you pick them, with dark glass cards that rise in and short words that scan at a glance. Pick the look, where the tabs go, Roomy or Compact and a colour, or the whole colour wheel, gradients and grain, with a live preview; bring Arc's spaces or another browser's things over; set up sync, the blocker and its lists, and the default browser; and finish on the six keys worth knowing. ## Habits from Arc and Chrome

- **Your history on your other devices**: with sync on, where you've been on one Mac shows up in your others' history and address bar, and in Quo for iPhone's, the last 60 days, through your own iCloud or folder, and only once another device of yours has shown up. Clear History, or forgetting a page, clears it everywhere, while a page visited since stays. On unless turned off (Settings › General › Sync history); private tabs are never kept, and a space with its own sign-ins keeps its history on its Mac. *Nov 2026*
- **A strong password for a new account**: under a sign-up's password box, or a new one when you change it, "Use Strong Password" offers one as Safari makes them (three runs of six letters and digits, a capital and a digit among them), and a click puts it in the password box and the box that asks for it again. Send the form and Quo offers to save it, as any password. Never under a box for the password you have now. *Nov 2026*
- **Your cards at checkout**: add a card in Settings › Passwords, and under a checkout's number, name or expiry box it's offered as "Visa •••• 4242"; after Touch ID its number, name and expiry go in, the expiry as the form wants it (MM/YY, or a month and a year in their lists), and the caret goes to the security code's box for you, as the code is never kept. The number lives in the Mac's keychain; beside it only the kind, the last four digits, the name and the expiry. A card's boxes inside a payment provider's own frame (Stripe's) aren't filled. *Nov 2026*
- **Handoff**: the page in front is offered to your other devices, as Safari's is, so it's in the app switcher on your iPhone (Quo for iPhone, or Safari without it); and a page from another device comes here once Quo is your browser. Never a private tab. *Nov 2026*
- **Listen to Page**: in the View menu or ⌘K, the article read aloud by the Mac's best voice for its language, a paragraph at a time, the text you've selected if any; a small bar at the foot of the page pauses, goes on and stops, and going elsewhere in the tab stops it. *Nov 2026*
- **A right-click on the column has the space's menu**: on the space under the tabs, the band at the top or round the foot's buttons, New Tab, then the space's own menu as its button at the foot has it (your spaces, New Space…, Rename, Icon, Colour…, Tidy Tabs, Downloads), with Hide Sidebar and Sidebar Settings… at the end. Without spaces, as before. *Nov 2026*
- **Perplexity and ChatGPT as your search engine**: both are in Settings › Browsing › Search with, and in Quo for iPhone's Search Engine, beside Google, DuckDuckGo, Bing, Ecosia, Startpage, Kagi, Brave Search and Qwant. ChatGPT is asked to search the web for the words. Neither has suggestions to give as you type, so with either one the field suggests your own tabs, history and bookmarks, and your words go to no other engine. *Nov 2026*
- **The Library, past your first space**: two fingers to the right from your first space bring in a Library as Arc's, Downloads first, with Media (the pictures and videos you've downloaded, as thumbnails), Spaces, Bookmarks and Archived Tabs down a rail beside it. It follows the fingers the whole way, the column widening by the rail and the page giving way as it comes, and when they let go carries on at their speed and lands without a wobble; a short, slow stroke goes back, a quick one carries. Two fingers back, the arrow at its foot, or the Library button at the column's foot (where the bookmarks button was) put it away. The window's lights sit at the rail's top with each section's title beside them. In the column on the left; the column of icons and the column on the right keep the bookmarks button. *Nov 2026*
- **Writing Tools in a page's text boxes**: right-click in anything you can type in on the web, an email, a comment box, a doc, and Apple's Writing Tools are first in the menu, Proofread, Rewrite, Make Friendly or Professional, Summarize and the rest, rewriting in place as they do in Safari and Mail, on a Mac with Apple Intelligence. WebKit leaves them out of other apps' menus, and Search had none. *Nov 2026*
- **New groups name themselves**: a group you make gets a name from its tabs, "Tokyo trip" or "Recipes" where it would have been "Group 3", by Apple Intelligence on this Mac, and is named again as tabs join or leave until you give it a name of your own, which always stays. If you're typing a name when it arrives, yours wins. Settings › Tabs › Name new groups. *Nov 2026*
- **Search your history by what it was about**: in History, "japan trip" finds the Tokyo hotels and flights you looked at, "home loan" the mortgage calculator, "dinner ideas" the recipes, under the pages that match your words outright, as Close in meaning. Worked out on this Mac with macOS's own word embeddings, in a few milliseconds, nothing sent anywhere, on any Mac, no Apple Intelligence needed. Search only finds the exact words. *Nov 2026*
- **Hold Shift over a link to see what it says**: a card at the foot of the page with the page's title, its site and two or three sentences on what it says, before you open it, as Arc's 5-second previews did. The page is fetched once, without your cookies or sign-ins and through the proxy when one is set; Apple Intelligence summarises it on the Mac, and without it the card shows the page's own description. A PDF or a page that isn't there says so. Never on a plain hover, and off in Settings › Browsing. *Nov 2026*

- **Go to an address you select**, as in Chrome: right-click razegrowth.com chosen on a page and Go to razegrowth.com opens it in a new tab, above Search with. Words that aren't an address, or an email address, aren't offered it. *Nov 2026*
- **Bringing things over says what it's doing**: extensions coming over from another browser show a card with which one, how far along (2 of 7) and whether it is downloading, being made ready or waiting for your answer, then what came of it. Rows with nothing to bring are switched off, Arc's spaces and pins are a row of Arc's choices rather than a card of their own, and browsers macOS keeps from Quo share one card with a button each. *Nov 2026*
- **Another browser's profiles and extensions come over too**: Bring Things Over, in the onboarding and in Settings, now offers Profiles and Extensions beside passwords, bookmarks and history, each with a count. Every profile of Chrome, Dia, Arc, Brave, Edge, Vivaldi, Firefox, Zen and the rest comes into a profile of the same name here with its own bookmarks and history (its first into the window you're in, one with nothing in it left out, one already here found by name). - **⌥⌘← and ⌥⌘→ walk your spaces**, as in Arc, beside ⌃1–⌃9 to jump to one. Settings no longer calls ⌘1–⌘9 for spaces Arc's way: Arc keeps spaces on ⌃ and tabs on ⌘, as Quo does by default. *Nov 2026*

- **A pin keeps its site, and what leaves it opens in a new tab**: a link in a pin to another site, or another site's address typed into it, opens a new tab beside it, as Dia does, and the pin stays where it was. ## AI agents

- **Agents on your Mac can control the whole browser over MCP**: with Settings › About › Developer › Give agents full control on, Claude Code, Codex or any other agent you allow can do what you do in Quo. That means every window, space, group, pin and tab (open, close, select, pin, rename, mute, split, group, move between spaces and windows), every menu command by its id, bookmarks, history, downloads and settings, and pages themselves: any tab's, with your sign-ins, read, clicked, typed into, scrolled, pictured, or run a script in. The MCP server is Quo itself, so there's nothing else to install: Add to Claude Code in Settings, or `claude mcp add quo -- /Applications/Quo.app/Contents/MacOS/Quo --mcp`, and Quo starts in the background if it isn't open. It speaks the current MCP revision (2026-07-28) and the earlier ones. Each agent still asks in the window first, says it wants full control, and stops at Stop. It never sees saved passwords, card numbers or one-time codes (its scripts read those fields as empty, can't turn them into text fields or send their form elsewhere), never private tabs, and can't change its own switches or the agents you always allow. Clearing history, deleting a space, or closing several tabs, a group or a window at once asks you first. Off by default, and offered in onboarding, off. Search let agents work only in tabs of their own, through a separate Python script in its repository. *Nov 2026*

## Blocking

- **Popular filter lists a click away** (EasyList, EasyPrivacy, Fanboy's Annoyances, the cookie notice list). *Nov 2026*
- **Filter lists for the built-in blocker**: the lists every other blocker uses, by address or file, compiled into WebKit's own rules. Search closed this ([driceroland/Search#738](https://github.com/driceroland/Search/pull/738)). *Oct 2026*

## Extensions

- **Extension icons follow light and dark on the fly**: an extension's hidden offscreen page now hears Quo switch between light and dark, as Chrome's does, so a toolbar button drawn for each (Glance's eye, for one) changes with the window instead of keeping the look it started with until the extension is reloaded. *Nov 2026*
- **Extensions that slow pages down are offered On click**: each extension's handlers for the pointer and scrolling, and its change observers, are timed in the pages it runs in. One that has taken more than two seconds of the pages' time in a quarter of an hour, while running on every site, gets a card at the side of the window saying so, with Run on Click, Not Now and Don't Ask Again; never a password manager, one already limited, or one without a button. A new install that is plainly a tool, a button with scripts on every site and a name that says SEO, fonts, an inspector or a colour picker, starts On click and says so. Search ran every extension in every page and said nothing about what they cost; in Gmail, two such tools took a third of the page's time on the pointer alone. *Nov 2026*
- **Agent extensions can work the page, not just read it**: Claude, ChatGPT's agent and any other extension that asks for Chrome's `debugger` can click, drag, type, press keys (Return submits, Tab moves on, ⌘A selects all), scroll, take screenshots and run scripts in a tab, as they do in Chrome. The clicks and keys are real events handed to the page, never to Quo's own menus, and your pointer doesn't move. Search refused the debugger outright, so these extensions could read a page but every action failed or silently did nothing. *Nov 2026*
- **Extensions hear about their errors**: a failed call now sets `chrome.runtime.lastError` for its callback, as in Chrome. Before, every failure looked like success to an extension written with callbacks. *Nov 2026*
- **Lighter on every page**: the part of Quo's extension helper that content scripts carry is about 120 KB, not 275 KB, in every frame. 1Password's buttons and menus feel smoother. *Nov 2026*
- **Popups open at their settled size** instead of opening small and jumping (Ahrefs, Cookie-Editor), or growing and morphing as they open. *Nov 2026*
- **Notifications work as in Chrome**: an extension's buttons under the notification's Options, and clicks, buttons and dismissals going back to the extension. *Nov 2026*
- **⌘-drag the pinned extension buttons to reorder them**, as the Mac's menu bar does. *Nov 2026*

## Video

## Usage numbers

- **Quo counts how many people use it, and nothing more**: once a day it tells TelemetryDeck it was opened, with its version and the Mac's or iPhone's model and OS, as a random number hashed on the device; never a page, an address or a search. Off in Settings › Privacy (Settings on the iPhone), and the number goes with it. Search counts nothing, and a build without an app ID, Search's own included, still sends nothing. *Nov 2026*

## Fixes

- **Finishing the onboarding no longer asks you to bring Arc over twice**: with Arc's spaces and pins chosen, Start Browsing now brings them straight into the sidebar and says what came. Before, it opened Bring Things Over with every switch on and Bring them in waiting, which looked like nothing had come over yet. *Nov 2026*
- **Icons in menus are back**: macOS 27 hides a menu item's icon unless it asks to be shown, so the spaces' icons, the Icon menu's choices and bookmarks' site icons had all gone. *Nov 2026*
- **Bring Things Over finds Chrome on a Mac where it is the only browser**: recent macOS keeps Chrome's data folder from other apps until you allow it, and the onboarding then said no other browser was on the Mac at all. It now names Chrome (or any browser macOS keeps this way, wherever its app is) with an Allow Access button that opens its folder for one click, and Settings points to Full Disk Access for good. *Nov 2026*
- **Extension badges sit in the middle**: a count was a little low and off to one side, and three or four characters made a wide slab across the icon. Now a circle for one character, a short capsule for more, in a condensed cut for three or four, cut at four as Chrome does. *Nov 2026*
- **A page from the small window for links keeps its address**: Open in Quo moved it into the row with an empty address bar until it was reloaded, and the small window's own bar never named the site. *Nov 2026*
